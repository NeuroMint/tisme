# Interview Guide

## How to record

Use the iPhone **Voice Memos** app — reliable, high quality, one tap to
start. Name each recording by topic (e.g. "Parents and Childhood") and date,
and drop the file in `archive/01-autobiography-recordings/`.

Optional later upgrade: Otter.ai or MacWhisper for automatic transcription.
Not needed to start.

Don't try to answer every question below in one sitting — one topic per
session is plenty. Start with: *"Tell me about your parents and what they
were like."*

## ~40 starter questions

**Family foundations**
1. What do you know about the earliest Perry ancestors?
2. What stories did your parents tell you about their own childhood?
3. What were your parents like as people?
4. What values did they try to teach you?
5. What is your earliest childhood memory?
6. What was your home like growing up?
7. What traditions or routines did your family have?
8. Were there any family sayings or beliefs that stayed with you?

**Childhood and school**
9. What kind of child were you?
10. What did you enjoy most at school?
11. Who were your closest friends growing up?
12. Were there teachers who influenced you strongly?
13. What did you think your future might look like at that time?

**Early independence**
14. What made you choose your university course?
15. Looking back, was that decision planned or accidental?
16. What was university life like socially and intellectually?
17. What were the big cultural influences at the time (music, politics, ideas)?

**Early career**
18. How did you get your first real job?
19. What did you learn from those early jobs?
20. When did you first realise the importance of professional relationships?

**Connections and networking**
21. Which people had the biggest influence on your career?
22. When did you realise that connections open doors?
23. Can you remember a job opportunity that came entirely through someone you knew?

**Crossroads and fate**
24. Looking back, what were the biggest turning points in your life?
25. Were there moments where a small decision changed everything?
26. How did you meet your wife?
27. Do you believe chance plays a role in life success?

**International life**
28. What led you to work in Canada?
29. What surprised you most about living there?
30. What eventually led you to Australia?

**Career journey**
31. What industries did you work in over your career?
32. Which companies no longer exist today?
33. How did technology change those industries?

**Leadership and culture**
34. What makes a strong organisational culture?
35. What mistakes do companies often make about culture?
36. What leadership challenges did you face during the pandemic?

**Reflection**
37. What personal strengths helped you succeed?
38. What weaknesses caused difficulties?
39. What advice would you give someone starting their career today?
40. What values do you most hope your grandchildren inherit?

## Memory Trigger Method

When memory goes blank chronologically, trigger it with sensory anchors
instead:

- **People** — parents, grandparents, school/uni friends, first boss,
  mentors, colleagues. Who influenced you most? Challenged you most? Opened
  doors for you?
- **Places** — childhood home, school, university, first office, countries
  lived in. What did it feel like arriving? Who did you meet there?
- **Objects** — passports, photos, diaries, letters, awards, clippings,
  career test reports. Pick one up and see what it brings back.
- **Music** — songs from different life periods are one of the strongest
  anchors.
- **Firsts** — first job, promotion, overseas move, failure, leadership
  role, crisis managed.

Keep a running note on your phone called **"Stories I Must Tell."** Whenever
a memory pops up unprompted, jot a one-line title. That list becomes the
backbone of the book — don't try to organise it perfectly, just capture it
while it's fresh.
