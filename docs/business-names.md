# Business / Platform Name — Decision

**Decided: Tisme**, with tagline **"Tisme — My Life. My Story."**

## How the decision was made

The shortlist started as seven names (confirmed available by Graham):
Tisme, Merror, Ancestory, Lifemine, Miography, Ourstory, Legasee. That
narrowed to a debate between the two standouts, **Tisme** and **Legasee**,
worked through in a dedicated branding chat and settled with Tisme winning.

**Legasee** — reads instantly as "legacy," clear and heritage-focused, but
felt (to Graham) formal, traditional and a little slow/institutional —
closer to an archive or genealogy service than a personal story.

**Tisme** — read as "'tis me" → "it is me," which fits an autobiography
perfectly. Less immediately clear on first hearing, but that's the same
trade-off made by Google, Apple, Spotify, Airbnb: an invented/abstract name
that the brand experience fills with meaning over time. It reads as modern,
personal and informal rather than archival.

Run through five standard naming tests (clarity, memorability, emotional
resonance, conversational fit, expansion potential), plus two informal
checks — the **Kitchen Table Test** ("would a family naturally say this at
home?") and the **Tattoo Test** ("does it carry enough emotional weight to
last a lifetime?") — Tisme won on all but raw first-hearing clarity, which
the tagline solves directly:

- **Tisme** = identity, voice, the person
- **Legasee** = archive, heritage, the record

Autobiography is about the person, not the archive, so Tisme fits the
project better. The tagline **"My life. My story."** does double duty: it
explains the name for anyone who doesn't immediately parse "'tis me," and
it anchors the emotional register (personal ownership, not institutional
record-keeping).

Bonus: unlike Legasee, Tisme has a plausible path to becoming a verb/noun
in family use ("Have you Tismed your story yet?" / "Grandpa left us his
Tisme"), the kind of linguistic adoption only a handful of brands achieve.
